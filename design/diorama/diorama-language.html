The UI is part of the show

Persona 5's UI lead says he went from "laying out information" to treating UI as graphical entertainment. The team kept one dominant red and almost no other colours.

GamesRadar · PC GamerOne colour owns each screen.

Clarity beats cool, every time

Into the Breach: "we would sacrifice cool ideas for the sake of clarity every time." Every unit shows exactly what it is about to do.

Game DeveloperIn a match the style steps back and the numbers step forward.
3

A typeface is a personality

Splatoon's UI designers drew their own font, and it went everywhere: in game, on the web, in ads, on merchandise. It shaped the game's character.

Nintendo (JP) · UI Crunch talkOne display face, Chakra Petch Bold, used huge and everywhere: menu, match, site and store page.
4

Menus and game are one thing

Balatro gives its menus the same treatment as its cards, and its feedback is fast and physical: cards slam, numbers tick up. Nothing breaks the spell.

Design guide · IndieklemThe menu is built from the match's own pieces.
5

Put the interface in the world

Dead Space set out to keep every interaction inside the fiction. Diegetic UI makes the world feel real instead of overlaid.

WaylineThe menu is a diorama of the battlefield.
6

Low poly works when it's simple

Bad North is praised for "simple readable graphics": bold team colours against stark enemies, and a low-poly style that matches the simple rules.

Pocket Tactics · GodIsAGeekFlat fields, honest models, no gloss.
7

Deadpan beats wacky

Untitled Goose Game sets a clean sans and a typewriter face in flat colour, like a storybook. The jokes come from the goose, never from the menus.

02 · Your five points

How the language says them

Strategic depth

Notes point at the board like a tactics diagram: a dot on the unit, a line, a typed label. In the Units view they carry real numbers ("1100 health · 108 damage a second · 2 m reach"). How to play tilts the camera straight down to the plan view, so the zones and the grid are the explanation.

Low poly, not triple-A

The hero image is the game's own models, live, from models3d.js. No paintings, renders or trailers. Everything around it is flat colour with paper grain: no gloss, chrome, glass or glow. The models carry the "3D", so the UI doesn't have to.

Goofy, not cartoonish

Deadpan. The type is square and stern and the notes are typewritten, but what they say is "Just a horse. Unit · costs gold · nobody knows why." The ent sleeps through the menu. The UI never bounces, wobbles or uses speech bubbles; the world is allowed to.

Serious artists

Confident, poster-like composition: huge type, empty space, one thing at a time. The soundtrack is credited like a film's ("Original soundtrack · Reveal by Snetänd") on every screen, never hidden in Settings.

Ages 12 to 50

Huge words to tap. Contrast is cream on ultramarine (about 9:1) and ink on saffron (about 10:1). Plain words, and the camera shows what each choice is before you pick it. No reading needed to find Play: it's the biggest thing on the screen.

Units

The shard

A hand-cut polygon of four to six corners sits behind whatever has focus. It is never a rectangle, and no two in a row share a shape: a low-poly brush stroke. Saffron on dark fields, ink on saffron. It snaps in with a small overshoot (180 ms).

Just a horse.
Unit

The note

A dot and ring on the thing, an elbow line, a typed label, and a saffron tag for the kind (reward, unit, rule). Notes follow their thing as the camera moves. They replace cards, tooltips and banners.

The move

Navigation is a camera move over the island: Units zooms on a unit, How to play goes straight down, Rankings visits the enemy keep. The field colour wipes (450 ms). Nothing ever waits for an animation to finish.

05 · Rules that keep it right

Do this, not that

No boxes around content. Words sit on the field and notes sit on the world. A panel exists only where text must sit over the busy 3D board in a match, and then it is flat ink. Cards in a grid.

One field, one shard, one big word. Each screen has a single colour, a single focused thing and a single word bigger than the rest.

The world does the jokes. The UI reports them plainly in typewriter. Exclamation marks, emoji, bouncing buttons.

Real numbers. Stats are printed, not hidden behind stars or vague words. Depth shows by being readable.

In a match, clarity first. The field becomes the board; the display type shrinks to round numbers and the Fight button. Notes show ranges and targets before anything happens.

Flat, matte and grainy. Gradients, gloss, glass blur, glow, rounded pills. The models are the only 3D.

Credit the artists. Composers on every screen with music; model and art credits in the unit notes.

Fast. Shards snap (180 ms), fields wipe (450 ms), cameras glide (0.6 s), and every tap works at once. Reduced motion keeps the camera cuts and drops the glides.
