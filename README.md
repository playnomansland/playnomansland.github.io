# No Mans Land

A small auto-battler for the phone browser. Buy squads, place them, and watch the armies fight. Play the AI or challenge a friend live.

- **Tutorial:** four guided rounds of one match that build the screen up step by step: your deployment zone blinking, your tower ("Protect it"), one card, a tile, Fight. The enemy's hidden Skeleton Archers win round 1 (how damage works); in round 2 their army is in sight and Fire Mage, which beats crowds, wins it. Round 3 opens up a little: the tower pick (Bastion or Keep only, a line on each; `m.towerOffer` fixes the offer) and its skill (a glowing panel button), a shop of five basic units with the deployment cap (the enemy adds a hidden Brute). Round 4: a level-up (its sheet also has Move, Sell and skills), the flanks and no man's land. Then the enemy plays for real, as the AI, with the full shop and the clock, and one coach line on that first screen says so. Tutorial fights play at 1x; the X on the coach line leaves. The menu leads with it until it's been played through round 4 (saved on the device), then it's a small button. The script is `TUT` and `panelTutorial()` in `src/site-template.html`; `tools/tests/tutorial-ui.js` checks it on both boards. Rounds 1 and 2 come out the same wherever the player places (checked headlessly over the zone's tiles), so after changing those four units' stats, check the script still holds.

## How live matches work

- **On the game server (since 2026-11-02, server step 3):** friends' matches on the built-in units and tournament matches are refereed by the game server (`server/src/room.js`, one Durable Object per match code, WebSocket `/room/<CODE>`; a tournament match is `/room/<CODE>?t=<TOURNAMENT>`). Both phones are equal players (`mode = 'net'`, `me` = the side the server gives: the one who made the match, or a tournament match's host, is side 0). Links are `?play=CODE`.
- **Actions, not state:** every Game call a player makes on their own side that works (`Referee.ACTIONS` in `src/referee.js`: place, move, sell, level, skill, unlock, package and tower picks, loan, slot spin, walls, Mark, and the rest) is also sent as `{ t: 'act', seq, fn, a }` (the wrapper after `SFX_ACTS`, `srvAct`). The server runs the same Game function on its own match for that side (`Referee.act`: only listed functions, only the listed argument kinds, only while deploying and not Ready), so a modified page can only do what the rules allow. Each action is acked with the server's fingerprint of that side (`Referee.sideHash`); a phone out of step asks for a sync. A phone plays its actions at once on its own copy and keeps them as pending until acked; a view from the server replaces the copy and the pending ones are played again on top (`srvReplay`). Undo all is an action too (`undoAll`: back to the same point as on the phone, `Referee.sideState` / `restoreSide`, which the page uses as well).
- **Hidden info is kept on the server:** each phone (and each spectator) only gets its view (`Referee.view` / `hide`): the opponent's squads, levels, skills, sales, moves, unlocks, walls, Mark, tower buys and picks of this round, their gold and extra deployment as the round began, and in round 1 their leader, packages, buff tiles and spins. The fight (`{ pre, post, summary }`, `Referee.fight`) shows everything, as before.
- **Clock and flow on the server:** the deploy clock (90 s, or the tournament's) is a Durable Object alarm; it waits for tower picks, the leader draft (each pick has `Referee.DRAFT_PICK_MS`, 20 s, then `draftClock` picks the first leader left) and for a player who is away (up to 2 minutes, `AWAY_MAX_MS`), and the fight starts 0.5 s after it runs out (`GRACE_MS`). Ready, next round (a player who is away moves on by themselves) and rematch go through the server. A reloaded phone gets its side back with its key (`phonebellum.net.v1`, the ?play= link or Back to match on the menu). A tournament match starts by itself once both players are in and the server sends the result to the tournament (`TourRoom.serverResult`); phones don't report it.
- **Pick clock (2026-11-10):** the round 2 leader pick and the round 3 tower pick count down on the pick screen (`#pick-clock`: big seconds and a bar that runs down; red and pulsing for the last 10 s; the Hide button's label carries the seconds too). 20 s a pick (`Referee.TOWER_PICK_MS`, and `DRAFT_PICK_MS` for each leader pick, whoever's turn it is). On the server `towerClock` (beside `draftClock` in `src/referee.js`) gives a player who hasn't picked in time the first tower on offer, and `sync` / `status` carry `pick` (ms left, `Referee.pickLeft`); the page sends the card its player tapped just before. Vs the computer and phone to phone each page runs its own player's clock (`pickTick` in `src/site-template.html`) and picks the tapped card, or else the first leader left / the first tower. No pick clock where there's no deploy clock (vs the computer with the timer off, the tutorial, puzzles) or in 2v2. The deploy clock still waits for the picks, so pick time never eats deploy time. Checks: `node tools/tests/referee.js` (clock section).
- **Phone to phone (PeerJS), the fallback:** Factions matches, Counter Lab units, `?peer=1`, old `?join=` links, the lobby's Phone to phone instead button, a typed code nobody made on the server (`nomatch`), and a new match when the server can't be reached at all. A tournament goes phone to phone when its `net` setting is `peer`. Phones then connect over WebRTC using [PeerJS](https://peerjs.com) and its free public matchmaking server; the host's phone runs the match and trusts what the guest sends. The host's match is saved on the host's device.
- The battle engine only uses arithmetic that gives identical results in every browser (and on the server), so both phones play back the same fight.
- Each round has a deploy countdown of 90 seconds. The fight starts when it runs out, or earlier when both players tap Ready.
- Tests: `node tools/tests/referee.js` (headless: whole matches on every map with every leader, phones and server in step, playback the same, cheating refused, the clock), `cd server && npm test` (the same over WebSockets against the local server, reconnects, tournament results), `node tools/tests/server-match-ui.js` (two pages in the browser), `node tools/tests/server-history-ui.js` (match history from the server), `node tools/tests/account-ui.js` (the Account sheet), `node tools/tests/prepare-units.js` (the server's units are the page's).
- **Starting units (2026-11-07):** in round 1 each player sees which units the opponent's package squads are (`Game.startSeen(m, side)`: the package's `squads`, or `m.packSeen[side]` where the screen doesn't have the package), never where they stand, the leader or the package itself. The server's view (`Referee.hide`) and the PeerJS guest's (`syncState`, plus `start` on the host's `status`, sent when it picks) carry `m.packSeen` (`UNDO_SKIP`, so `sideHash` is unchanged). The page lays them out (`foeStart()` in `boardView`: `[{ unit, x, y }]`, side by side along the back row of their zone, away from their tower; not in 2v2, tutorial, sandbox or for spectators) and both boards draw them see-through with a label (render2d `drawFoeStart`, render3d `collectDeploy` with look `ghost` and a `label|` sprite). Check: `node tools/tests/start-units-ui.js` (AI, and a PeerJS match when the peer server is up).
- **The AI presses Ready (2026-11-07):** against the AI (mode `ai`) there is no Fight / Fight now button: the player has the online Ready / Not ready button (`fightBtn()` is false only there; tutorial, puzzles, Replay a round and Claude keep Fight). `aiReadyTick` (from `tick`) sets `ready[1]` `AI_READY_MS` (5000) after the player can deploy: the count restarts while a package, tower or leader pick is open or the AI waits on the player's leader pick (`aiWaits`), unless the player is already ready. Both ready (`toggleReady` or `aiReadyTick`) runs the fight; the clock still ends the deploy as before. `boardView().foeReady` and `dragonCheck` follow `ready[foe()]` there, so the looks' opponent-ready scenery (Forest Glade explorer, Savanna vultures, Ashlands dragon) plays against the AI. Under `?test` the AI is ready at once unless `?aiready=ms`, so the UI tests click `#ready` where they clicked `#fight`. Check: `node tools/tests/ai-ready-ui.js`.
- **Fight speed:** both phones play a fight at the same speed. A player's pick (1×, 1.5×, 2×) is what they want, and the fight plays at the slower of the two picks: a faster pick is a request ("Opponent wants 2×", that button glowing on the other phone) that takes hold on both once the other picks it too; a slower pick slows both at once. Picks carry over to the next fights of the match; a new match starts at 1×. Against the computer the buttons just set the speed. (`liveSpeed`, `playSpeed`, `showSpeed` in `src/site-template.html`.)
- **Switching away mid-fight** (another tab or app) pauses the replay where it is; coming back goes on from there. In a live match the other player waits on the results screen meanwhile (the next round needs both), so after 45 s away (`LIVE_AWAY`) the rest of the fight plays out to its result, as before. `tools/tests/playback-mp.js` checks both.
- **Fair turns:** the engine gives neither side the edge in a tie. The side whose bodies act first alternates every tick; both sides move from where everyone stood when the tick began; a body killed this tick still attacks; heals, lifesteal, Glut, range cuts, knockback, stealth reveals and a fallen tower's penalty take hold once the tick's attacks have landed; side 1's squads stand mirrored (front row first); each side draws dodges and random heals from its own stream. Mirrored armies come out as exact mirrors (`node tools/tests/fairness.js`); before, the host won about 60% of mirrored duels that weren't draws.

## Game server

## Tournaments (invite only)

- **Start screen → Host a tournament.** The game server keeps the tournament (`server/src/tour.js`, one Durable Object per tournament code, WebSocket `/tour/<CODE>`; the rules are `src/tournament.js`, global `Tour`, which runs in node and on the server). ## Bridge map and terrain

The Bridge map (only when picked, never random) was made in the map editor: the Test Grid's board, towers, treasuries, flanks and tuning, with two rivers (columns 5 and 8, rows 6 to 11) and a raised bridge between them. Its editor JSON is kept as it came out of the editor in `EDITOR_MAPS` (`src/game.js`) and hangs on the map as `terrain`; another editor map goes in the same way (its buildings must still be on the game's own spots for now). What the terrain does (`TERRAIN`, `terrainTile`, `terrainOf` in `src/game.js`; the terrain section of `createBattle` in `src/engine.js`):

- **Walls** (`#`) stop walking, shots and sight; **water** (`~`) stops walking only (shots and sight pass over it); **shallow water** (`s`) slows whoever wades in it by 30%; a **bush** (`b`) hides whoever stands in it (seen from 3 m, or from inside a bush within a tile) and stops sight through it further than a tile from the one looking. Nothing can be placed on a wall or water tile. A tile with a form from the editor (line, corner, diagonal, edge wall) has only that much of it. Objectives and mushroom tiles aren't played yet.
- **Walking round things:** the engine works on a grid of 2 m cells (3 x 3 a tile). A walking body checks the next 9 m of its way; while it is clear it walks as before, and when a wall or water is in the way it follows the shortest way round (a path over the cell grid, kept per goal across fights) toward the furthest point of it it can walk to in a straight line, so its heading swings round in a smooth curve to the nearest opening. A body pushed into a blocked cell (by allies, knockback, a drop) steps back out.
- **Sight:** an enemy behind a wall can't be spotted, picked or shot. A body with no enemy in sight keeps marching, and once nothing is left ahead it walks round to the nearest one it can't see, without stopping for it. Lines of sight are worked out per pair of cells once and kept, so walls cost very little: AI matches on a walled version of the map ran at about 0.3 ms a fight tick, as fast as open ground.
- **Height:** a level per tile, −2 to +2. A body on higher ground than its target gets +1 m range and +2% damage per level of difference (`Engine.HEIGHT`), so +2 shooting at −2 gets +4 m and +8%. Shooting uphill is the opposite: −1 m range and −2% damage per level below the target (range never drops under 1 m). Flying units ignore height (and walls and water).
- **Drawing:** both boards draw the water, walls, shallow water and bushes on the floor, and height as light and shade with the slopes lit. `Game.groundHeight` gives the drawn height (3 m a level): it slopes smoothly between the middles of walkable tiles, so raised ground meets the open ground round it in a ramp, while water sits 1 m lower with a bank on its side. The 3D board raises its floor by it, stands models, rings, markers and shots on it, and draws wall tiles as stone blocks.

**The bridge objective** (the map's `objective`, `objectiveOf` / `objectiveTiles` in `src/game.js`, the objective in `createBattle`): the zone is the raised ground (the 16 tiles of the bridge and its ramps). In a fight, the side with more units standing on it (structures, drones and constructs don't count) holds it; holding it 5 s in a row wins it, once a fight, and its winner gets +50 gold for the next round (`summary.objective`, listed on the results screen as "bridge held"). A tie stops the clock and a new leader starts from zero. The board outlines the zone in dashed gold (in the colour of whoever holds it in a fight, solid once won, with a bar for the time held), and the fight clock reads e.g. "bridge 4–3 · you 2.1/5 s", then "bridge won by you". In AI matches either side wins it about equally often.

`node tools/tests/terrain.js` checks the map, the objective, placement, AI matches on it (nobody ever stands in the water) and, on a small made-up grid, walking round a wall, walls blocking shots and the height bonus.

## Rush map, and maps from the map editor

Maps made in the map editor go into `EDITOR_MAPS` (`src/game.js`) as the editor's JSON, and `editorMap(id, json, extra)` makes their `MAPS` entry: the grid (columns, rows, zone depth; no man's land is what is left between the zones), the towers' and treasuries' tiles (`spots`, used by `towerSpot` and `treasurySpot`), the flank tiles when they differ from the game's edge columns (`flankSet`; `useGrid` switches `isFlank` to them, and `flankTiles(side)` lists them), the terrain when there is any, and the Test Grid's tuning for maps made on it. `extra` adds the name, texts and rules (the Bridge's objective). Both are only when picked, never random.

**Rush:** zones of 14 by 8 tiles with only 2 rows of no man's land between them, so the armies meet almost at once; the towers one column further in (column 9 / 4), the treasuries one column further out, and the two back corners of each half are flank tiles as well. `node tools/tests/editor-maps.js` checks every editor map's grid, towers, treasuries, flanks and no man's land against its JSON, and plays AI matches on it to the end.

## Shiv, Water Mage and bleed

- **Unit traits:** a unit's `trait: { name, text }` shows on its sheet as "Trait: name". Shiv: Apply bleed; Water Mage: Waterhealing; Steel Wizard: Steel armor (6 armor).
- **Either-or skills:** `skillOr: [[1, 2]]` on a unit means a player can own skill 2 or skill 3 of it, not both (`Game.skillOr`, `canBuySkill`); the sheet boxes them together with an OR between them. The Shiv, Water Mage, Steel Wizard and Earth Wizard have it.
- **Upgrade rows** (`upRow()` in `src/site-template.html`): every upgrade (unit skills, tower skills, mage generals, the loan, the Mecha Tower's bots, building skills, the altar) is one compact row: its name and button, then one line of what it does, cut short. Press and hold a row (or Enter on it) for the whole text and details (what it needs, who it's for, the price rule). Choices that rule each other out are boxed with an OR between them (`orGroup()`): either-or skills, the mage generals, a building's skill of the round.
- **Tap a unit in a fight** (`inspectCard()`): a card at the top left of the board shows that body's health now, level, hit, attacks a second, range, speed, armor or ward, how many of its squad are left, the upgrades its squad fights with (bought skills, Enzo's free one, an awakening; built-in ones left out), and what's on it (stunned, poisoned, bleeding, burning, slowed, summoning). A player's tower also shows their leader and what it does (as the tower's sheet does while deploying; not in 2v2, which has no leaders). On a PC with the Diorama match screen (`html.diorama.pcwide`) the card stands in the deploy panel's column under the fight's speeds and Skip, not on the name and health: `inspectCard()` measures its top from what the panel shows, `src/diorama.css` places the rest (the 2v2 artifact card, `.bcard.right`, goes under the opponent's side). It updates live and goes when the body falls, a tap elsewhere or its ✕. Stealthed enemies can't be picked.
- **Bleed** (`BLEED` in `src/engine.js`): a unit with `bleedEvery: 2` gives a stack of bleed with every second hit it lands on the same target. A bleeding body loses 3 health twice a second for 2.5 s (each new stack starts the 2.5 s again; 48 a tick at 5 stacks); every stack doubles that, up to 5 stacks; at 4 stacks it also loses 2% of its max health a second, and at 5 stacks 5% (`BLEED.pct`). Bleed goes past armor. The stacks are the target's own, so every source adds to them. Too deep (`bleedDeep`): 1 s longer, and no healing of any kind while it lasts. Anemia (`bleedStun`): a stun of 1.2 s when the stacks reach 5. Titanium spears give a stack too. Drawn as a red drop with a pip per stack (2D) or a red dot per stack (3D).

Pool shaper (`pools`): its attacks leave pools for the rest of the fight that never overlap; every second enemies in one take 25% of the hit, allies heal it and get +0.5 m/s for 2 s. Flow state (`flow`): +0.1 attacks a second and +0.4 m range per attack made standing still, up to 5; walking takes them away. ## Factions gamemode

A second gamemode on the standard map. Each faction is a small roster with its own theme, a trait and its own tower. Before the match the players draft factions one at a time, two each (A, B, B, A). Each player can then only unlock, buy and place units of their own two factions, and at round 3 they pick between their two factions' towers instead of the usual tower offer. Starting packages are dealt from your own factions' units, so give every faction at least one unit at 200 gold or less.

The mode unlocks on the start screen (Factions vs AI) and in the friend lobby (Standard / Factions) once there are enough factions for a draft (4 for now; each pick and ban needs its own).

Factions go in `src/factions.json`, then run `node tools/counter-table.js` and rebuild. Format:

```json
{
  "factions": [{
    "id": "deepsea", "name": "Deep Sea", "color": "#3FA7D6",
    "theme": "One line shown in the draft",
    "trait": { "name": "Crushing depths", "text": "+20% damage, -15% speed", "hit": 20, "speed": -15 },
    "tower": { "name": "Coral Spire", "text": "What it does", "unit": { "hp": 650, "hit": 30, "aps": 1.2, "range": 10 } },
    "units": [ { "name": "...", "cost": 100, "bodies": 4, "hp": 200, "...": "same fields as units in default-roster.json" } ]
  }],
  "skills": []
}
```

- `trait` changes all the faction's units (spawned ones too) by a percentage: `hp`, `hit`, `aps`, `range`, `speed`. It is optional.
- `tower.unit` overrides the standard tower's stats. With `drone: { orbit, unit }` (and `idle: true`) the tower doesn't shoot itself: a drone circles it `orbit` metres out and does the shooting (the Mecha Tower).
- `tower.awake`: the tower's stats while Core Awoken is on (the Mecha Tower drives and lobs mortar shells).
- `tower.rounds`: skills bought every round for that round's fight, each `{ id, name, cost, text, bots: { count, unit }, mines: { each, hit, radius } }` (the Mecha Tower's minebots and repairbots; a bot unit has `bot: "mine"` or `"repair"`).
- `skills`: skills only the factions use. Units can also use any skill from `default-roster.json` by its id.
- Unit names must be unique across all factions.
- The draft order, picks per player and the map are in `FACTION_MODE` in `src/game.js`. A ban phase later is a matter of adding `{ "who": "A", "act": "ban" }` steps to its order.
- `node tools/tests/factions.js` checks the draft and faction matches headlessly with six throwaway test factions.
- `node tools/tests/tutorial.js` checks headlessly that tutorial rounds 1 and 2 come out the same wherever the player places.
- `node tools/tests/standard-pack.js` checks the Standard pack (units, leaders, packages, towers), the bank's one action a round (a loan or the Major investment) and Frozen Peak with its extras off, headlessly.

### Factions – devtest

A test bed for the Factions mode while it's being designed: the Test Grid board, a draft where both players may take the same faction, no tower until round 3, a free pick every round (lesser buildings, the Powertower, Powers, neutral units), spells, arrows that guide a squad, and disguised units. vs AI only. The plan, who does what and the data formats are in [docs/FACTIONS-DEVTEST.md](docs/FACTIONS-DEVTEST.md); notes on unit awareness and targeting in [docs/AWARENESS.md](docs/AWARENESS.md).
- Until there are 4 factions the mode stays locked. To try a faction's units now: Counter Lab → Import units → "From <faction> (faction)", save, then Load units in the game.

Fight mechanics added for factions (they work for any unit, in Counter Lab too):

- `roll` (seconds): a roller. It never stops to attack; it rolls at its target from 1 m/s up to its `speed` over this many seconds, hits every enemy body it rolls over for its `hit`, carries on `ROLL_PAST` (9 m since 2026-11-07, was 6) past the target, stops and rolls back. `aps` and `range` only matter for the shop's numbers.
- `build`: `{ "time": 1.5, "range": 25, "count": 1, "unit": { ...stats } }`. Once per fight the unit stops when an enemy is within `range` − 1 m, builds `count` copies of `unit` (a tile apart) in `time` seconds and fights on. Builders building next to each other speed each other up (1×, 1.5×, 1.75×, …). What it builds never moves, levels with its builder, earns the builder's squad its XP, and doesn't count as a unit left standing.
- `skillNeeds`: `[null, null, [0, 1]]` = skill 3 can only be bought once skills 1 and 2 are owned (empty slots are skipped).
- Skill types: `momentum` (a roller gets up to speed faster), `link` (a squad's bodies share one health pool and fall together), `healbot` (a construct follows each squad and heals it a share of max health per second), `builder` (more constructs per build, faster builds, better constructs).
- Buildings are listed under `"buildings"` in `src/factions.json`: `{ id, name, faction, hp, buildSecs, selfBuild, survive, rebuild, levelCost, color, text, awake, skills }`. A player gets one in two ways:
  - a unit skill of type `building` (`"params": { "building": "factory" }`): buying it places the building on a free tile of your zone (the Factory);
  - a unit with `"building": "altar"` bought in the shop like any unit (the Altar of Build): a squad that never moves; `footprint: 2` makes it take a 2x2 block (its col, row are the block's first ones). Give it `idle`, `noCount` and `anchored` too, so it doesn't fight in Counter Lab; the counter table scores it 0.
- A building is a site until it is built: the nearest builder without a job builds it during fights (`buildSecs` seconds of work; a site nobody builds builds itself at `selfBuild` times that speed). The work carries over between fights; with `survive` a site still standing at the end of a fight it was worked on is built at once. A built building that falls starts the next round at `rebuild` %. Once built, one of its `skills` can be bought each round: `unit` (one unit made `time` s into the fight), `make` (`[[building, its skill, count], ...]`), `bless` (Blessing of the Cycle: `build`, `hp`, `speed`, `aps` %, `regen` for all your constructs) or `awake` (Core Awoken: your buildings get their kind's `awake` stats and walk; `awake.built` changes what builders build). What it makes earns it XP; it levels up with gold, and its units with it.
- New unit fields: `spinCap` (how far spin-up goes; default 3×), `spinHitCap` (how far damage spin-up goes; default 3×), `lockOn` (seconds before it attacks each new target after its first; the spin-up starts after it: the Sun Priest's 0.5 s), `tag` (its 3-letter tag on the board), `smooth` (no bobbing in 3D), `flee` (metres: keeps away from enemies that close, still shooting), `backline` (shoots the farthest enemy in reach), `lob` (shells fly over whoever is in the way and only land where aimed).

Paste it into the sheet's Apps Script, deploy it as a web app (Execute as: Me, Who has access: Anyone) and put the `/exec` URL in `HISTORY_URL` in `src/site-template.html` (already set to the live sheet; a new deployment gets a new URL, so update it then). ## Otto - The Gambler (slot machine)

Leader `otto` (All units only). `Game.SLOT` holds the numbers: 50 gold a spin, 3 a round, odds skull/shoe/coin 12%, goblin 3%, chest 1%. Outcomes come from `slotOutcome(m, side, round, k)` (seeded by the match, side, round and spin number), so both phones agree; `slotReels` gives the three symbols shown. `spin()` applies a spin here (gold, health floored at 1, the goblin squad: `gift: true`, level 1, unlocked, not counted as a deployment, can't be taken back). Horseshoes (since 2026-11-03) give, at random, +1 deployment or +1 free move every round for the rest of the match (`shoeKind`, `shoePerks`, `shoePerk`; the spin's record keeps `shoe`: 'deploy', 'move' or 'gold'); one of each at most, so the next horseshoe gives the other, and with both it pays `SLOT.shoeGold` (200). The host's `takeSpins` works out the same perks. Multiplayer: the guest sends `slots` (its spin count) and the host replays them with `takeSpins` for the health cost; the host's round 1 spins are hidden like its leader. A spin resets Undo all. Bot and Claude opponent: `aiSlots`. UI: `openSlots` / `doSpin` / `foeSlotNews` in `src/site-template.html`. Tests: `tools/tests/otto.js` (headless), `tools/tests/otto-ui.js` (browser).
