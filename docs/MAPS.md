## Test Grid map

A test map (Test Grid, only when picked, never random) on a bigger board: 14 columns by 18 rows, so each player's zone is 14 by 7 tiles instead of 12 by 6 (no man's land stays 4 rows). The towers are mirrored as on the Mushroom Cavern (`pointMirror`): the second player's tower stands on the flipped column.

Treasuries (the map's `treasury`, `TREASURY` in `src/game.js`): from round 1 each player has a treasury, 400 health, on the other side of their zone from their tower, one column in toward the middle and one row forward of the tower. Its tile is kept free from round 1 (drawn faintly until then). Like the tower it stands again at full health in every fight and doesn't count as army; losing it doesn't cripple anything. A fight it survives pays its owner 50 gold; if it is destroyed, the opponent steals 25% of the gold its owner left unspent at the fight, to the nearest 50, at least 50 and at most 200 (`treasurySteal`), instead (no pay that round). The results screen lists both. **The raid also shows on the board while the fight plays (2026-11-04):** when a treasury falls, the gold that changes hands floats up from it and fades — `+150 gold` in gold over the opponent's (you take it), `−150 gold` in red over your own. The page makes it in `goldFloat` (`absorb`, from `summary.treasury[side].lost`) as an effect of kind `gold` in `fx`, drawn by `drawGold` on the 2D board and by the text overlay (`showNote`) on the 3D one; it ages in real time, so it can be read at 2x as well. Tapping a treasury (yours or theirs) explains what it does. `tools/tests/treasury.js` checks the rules, `node tools/tests/treasury-gold-ui.js` the text on both boards (browser; needs `python3 -m http.server 8766`). The Factions devtest plays on this map too, so it has the treasuries as well. Units on it have 2 m more range (melee units, under 5 m, keep theirs), and units with a move speed of 3 or more move 0.2 m/s slower, 0.4 from 5 on; the Shiv and the Water Mage keep theirs (the map's `rangeAdd`, `slowSteps` and `tuneKeep`, applied by `mapTuned()`). The size is the map's `grid` in `MAPS` (`src/game.js`); `Game.useGrid(m)` puts the board on a match's grid, and every exported function that takes a match does it first, so all other maps keep the standard 12 by 16 board. The page's `syncGrid()` resizes the 2D board and starts the 3D board again when the grid changes.

## Bridge map and terrain

The Bridge map (only when picked, never random) was made in the map editor: the Test Grid's board, towers, treasuries, flanks and tuning, with two rivers (columns 5 and 8, rows 6 to 11) and a raised bridge between them. Its editor JSON is kept as it came out of the editor in `EDITOR_MAPS` (`src/game.js`) and hangs on the map as `terrain`; another editor map goes in the same way (its buildings must still be on the game's own spots for now). What the terrain does (`TERRAIN`, `terrainTile`, `terrainOf` in `src/game.js`; the terrain section of `createBattle` in `src/engine.js`):

- **Walls** (`#`) stop walking, shots and sight; **water** (`~`) stops walking only (shots and sight pass over it); **shallow water** (`s`) slows whoever wades in it by 30%; a **bush** (`b`) hides whoever stands in it (seen from 3 m, or from inside a bush within a tile) and stops sight through it further than a tile from the one looking. Nothing can be placed on a wall or water tile. A tile with a form from the editor (line, corner, diagonal, edge wall) has only that much of it. Objectives and mushroom tiles aren't played yet.
- **Walking round things:** the engine works on a grid of 2 m cells (3 x 3 a tile). A walking body checks the next 9 m of its way; while it is clear it walks as before, and when a wall or water is in the way it follows the shortest way round (a path over the cell grid, kept per goal across fights) toward the furthest point of it it can walk to in a straight line, so its heading swings round in a smooth curve to the nearest opening. A body pushed into a blocked cell (by allies, knockback, a drop) steps back out.
- **Sight:** an enemy behind a wall can't be spotted, picked or shot. A body with no enemy in sight keeps marching, and once nothing is left ahead it walks round to the nearest one it can't see, without stopping for it. Lines of sight are worked out per pair of cells once and kept, so walls cost very little: AI matches on a walled version of the map ran at about 0.3 ms a fight tick, as fast as open ground.
- **Height:** a level per tile, −2 to +2. A body on higher ground than its target gets +1 m range and +2% damage per level of difference (`Engine.HEIGHT`), so +2 shooting at −2 gets +4 m and +8%. Shooting uphill is the opposite: −1 m range and −2% damage per level below the target (range never drops under 1 m). Flying units ignore height (and walls and water).
- **Drawing:** both boards draw the water, walls, shallow water and bushes on the floor, and height as light and shade with the slopes lit. `Game.groundHeight` gives the drawn height (3 m a level): it slopes smoothly between the middles of walkable tiles, so raised ground meets the open ground round it in a ramp, while water sits 1 m lower with a bank on its side. The 3D board raises its floor by it, stands models, rings, markers and shots on it, and draws wall tiles as stone blocks.

**The bridge objective** (the map's `objective`, `objectiveOf` / `objectiveTiles` in `src/game.js`, the objective in `createBattle`): the zone is the raised ground (the 16 tiles of the bridge and its ramps). In a fight, the side with more units standing on it (structures, drones and constructs don't count) holds it; holding it 5 s in a row wins it, once a fight, and its winner gets +50 gold for the next round (`summary.objective`, listed on the results screen as "bridge held"). A tie stops the clock and a new leader starts from zero. The board outlines the zone in dashed gold (in the colour of whoever holds it in a fight, solid once won, with a bar for the time held), and the fight clock reads e.g. "bridge 4–3 · you 2.1/5 s", then "bridge won by you". In AI matches either side wins it about equally often.

`node tools/tests/terrain.js` checks the map, the objective, placement, AI matches on it (nobody ever stands in the water) and, on a small made-up grid, walking round a wall, walls blocking shots and the height bonus.

## Rush map, and maps from the map editor

Maps made in the map editor go into `EDITOR_MAPS` (`src/game.js`) as the editor's JSON, and `editorMap(id, json, extra)` makes their `MAPS` entry: the grid (columns, rows, zone depth; no man's land is what is left between the zones), the towers' and treasuries' tiles (`spots`, used by `towerSpot` and `treasurySpot`), the flank tiles when they differ from the game's edge columns (`flankSet`; `useGrid` switches `isFlank` to them, and `flankTiles(side)` lists them), the terrain when there is any, and the Test Grid's tuning for maps made on it. `extra` adds the name, texts and rules (the Bridge's objective). Both are only when picked, never random.

**Rush:** zones of 14 by 8 tiles with only 2 rows of no man's land between them, so the armies meet almost at once; the towers one column further in (column 9 / 4), the treasuries one column further out, and the two back corners of each half are flank tiles as well. `node tools/tests/editor-maps.js` checks every editor map's grid, towers, treasuries, flanks and no man's land against its JSON, and plays AI matches on it to the end.
